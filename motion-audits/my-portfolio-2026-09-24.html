The hero's two loops that never stop

A focused pass over the portfolio's hero — the shimmer headline, the background video, the six-part entrance, and the status dot — read through three motion practitioners' lenses, weighted for a creative portfolio front door.

Overall

The hero has real craft — one easing signature reused site-wide, GPU-cheap properties throughout, and unusually thorough prefers-reduced-motion coverage for a portfolio site. But its two signature moments never stop: the shimmer headline and the background video both loop forever with no way to pause either one, and all six entrance elements — from the H1 to a 10px scroll label — share one identical fade. Close the two loops first; then give the entrance some hierarchy.

Issues to address
CriticalJakub

The headline shimmers forever, with no way to stop it

WhatThe hero-shimmer keyframe runs 5s linear infinite on the H1 — every visitor, every visit, for as long as the tab stays open. No pause control, no one-shot fallback.

Why it mattersThis is the report's clearest break from "the best animation is that which goes unnoticed" — a shimmer that never resolves is, by definition, always noticed. It's also a WCAG 2.2.2 (Pause, Stop, Hide) case: moving content that auto-starts and lasts longer than five seconds needs a way to pause it.

Recommended motionPlay the shimmer once on mount (~1.1s ease-out), then let the headline settle into a static treatment. If it's worth repeating, retrigger it rarely — not continuously.

The background video autoplays, loops, and can't be paused

What<video autoPlay muted loop playsInline> behind the hero has no visible or programmatic pause control anywhere in the UI.

Why it mattersSame WCAG 2.2.2 requirement as the shimmer — auto-starting, looping content needs a pause affordance, independent of whether the visitor has prefers-reduced-motion set. Someone who finds ambient motion distracting while reading, but hasn't flipped an OS setting, currently has no way to stop it.

Recommended motionAdd a small, persistent pause/play toggle (bottom corner, same visual weight as the scroll indicator). Icon crossfades in ~150ms; the control itself needs no entrance flourish beyond materializing with the rest of the hero.

ImportantJakub

Six unrelated elements share one identical 800ms fade

WhatThe fadeUp factory (opacity 0→1, y 24→0, duration 0.8, same custom ease) is reused verbatim for the eyebrow, the H1, the paragraph, the CTA row, the domain-pill row, and the 10px scroll-indicator label — only delay changes.

Why it mattersThis is a textbook instance of the audit's "uniform-fade-in-on-every-element" pattern — the flagging threshold is four identical entrances; this hero has six. A 60-character headline and a 6-word scroll hint don't carry the same visual weight, so nothing in the motion currently communicates which element matters more.

Recommended motionKeep the shared easing curve — it's a good signature — but scale duration and translateY by importance: ~500ms/16px for the H1, ~350ms/8px for supporting text and pills, ~250ms/4px for the scroll hint.

  • 💡The enter recipe is opacity + translateY only — no blur. Through Jakub's lens

The vocabulary here is genuinely good — one easing signature, cheap properties, real reduced-motion coverage. What's missing is an ending: give the shimmer and the video a stop, and give the six-part entrance some hierarchy, and this crosses from "animated" to "polished."

Issues to address
ImportantEmil

A pulsing status dot sits next to the eyebrow, reporting no actual state

What<span className="h-1.5 w-1.5 animate-pulse ..."></span> runs Tailwind's default opacity pulse next to the "MOHAMED ALI JMAL" eyebrow — a "live" / "recording" style indicator attached to static text with nothing actually live behind it.

Why it mattersPulsing indicators are this audit's lowest-tolerance category — flag any instance, no frequency threshold required, because they're almost always attention-seeking decoration rather than functional status. Emil's question applies directly: should this animate at all? There's no state change to signal here.

Recommended motionDrop the loop. If the dot should still feel alive on load, give it a single ~200ms scale-in when the eyebrow mounts, then let it sit static — motion that announces arrival once, not a light that never turns off.

Which lens carried this audit

Referenced most

Jakub Krehel — Production Polish

The hero's problems are almost all finishing problems, not taste problems — two loops that never resolve, and one entrance treatment applied uniformly instead of with hierarchy. That's squarely Jakub's territory. Emil caught the one instance his zero-tolerance rule exists for; Jhey confirmed the restraint elsewhere is already correct and pointed at the one place worth spending more.

  • Lean EmilQuestion every loop before it ships — a status dot, a shimmer, a background video. If nothing is actually "live," the animation shouldn't run forever.
  • Lean JakubAudit every shared animation factory (like fadeUp) for uniformity — reused easing is good design-system hygiene, but reused duration and movement across elements of different importance isn't.
  • Lean JheyPick one element in the entrance — the domain pills are the obvious candidate — and give it a small stagger moment. Everywhere else, keep the restraint.
